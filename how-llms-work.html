How an AI language model works

Follow one path from raw internet text to the answer on your screen. Thirteen steps in three stages: collect the data, train the model, answer your prompt.

Step 1Collect the data

Crawl the web

Programs called crawlers visit web pages, follow every link they find, and save a copy of each page. Books, code, Wikipedia and research papers are often added too.

Common Crawl, a free public archive, has been saving the web since 2008 and holds hundreds of billions of pages. The open FineWeb dataset was built from 96 of its snapshots.

Step 2Collect the data

Clean it up

Most of the raw web is junk: spam, menus, cookie banners, and the same text copied thousands of times. A chain of filters keeps only real, useful writing.

Removing duplicates alone can throw away 45 to 75% of the raw data. After all filters, FineWeb still contained 15 trillion tokens of clean text.

Step 3Train the model

Build a vocabulary of tokens

Computers need numbers, not letters. A method called byte-pair encoding finds the pair of symbols that appears together most often and glues it into a new piece. Then it does it again, and again.

After tens of thousands of merges you get a vocabulary of roughly 50,000 to 200,000 tokens. Common words become one token, rare words get split into pieces.

Step 4Train the model

Pretraining: guess the next token

The model reads the text and, at every position, guesses which token comes next. Every wrong guess nudges billions of internal numbers, the weights, a tiny bit toward the right answer.

This one simple game, played trillions of times on thousands of GPUs for weeks or months, is where almost all of the model's knowledge comes from.

Step 5Train the model

The base model: a brilliant autocomplete

After pretraining, the model can continue any text in the style of the internet. But it doesn't know it's supposed to help you.

Ask it a question and it may just write more questions, because that's what a list of quiz questions on a web page looks like.

Step 6Train the model

Fine-tuning: learn to be an assistant

Next, the model trains on example conversations. Special tokens mark who is speaking, and the model learns to write the assistant's part.

It's the same next-token game as pretraining. Only the data changes: far smaller, and far higher quality.

Step 7Train the model

Learn from feedback

People compare two answers and pick the better one. A reward model learns their taste, and the assistant practices writing answers that score higher.

This is reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Newer variants skip the reward model (DPO), or reward answers that can be checked automatically, like math and code.

Step 8Answer your prompt

Your prompt becomes tokens

When you hit send, your message is wrapped in the chat format the model was trained on, cut into tokens, and turned into ID numbers. Try typing your own.

In English, a token is about three quarters of a word. Because the model sees pieces instead of letters, counting the r's in "strawberry" is harder than it looks.

Step 9Answer your prompt

Tokens become vectors

Each token ID picks one row from a giant table. That row is a list of numbers, called an embedding. Words with similar meanings get similar numbers.

GPT-2 used 768 numbers per token, today's large models use thousands. Position information is added so the model knows the word order.

Step 10Answer your prompt

Attention: words look at each other

Every token asks which earlier tokens matter to it and pulls in their information. That's how "it" works out that it means the animal. Click any word.

A token can only look backwards, never at words that come later. Many attention heads run side by side, each tracking a different kind of relationship.

Step 11Answer your prompt

Through the layers

Attention plus a small neural network (the MLP) make one block. The vectors flow up through dozens of these blocks, and each one refines what the model understands.

GPT-2 small has 12 blocks, GPT-3 has 96. The panel on the right is an illustration of how the best guess can sharpen with depth.

Step 12Answer your prompt

Pick the next token

The last layer gives every token in the vocabulary a score, and softmax turns the scores into probabilities. Then one token gets picked. Move the sliders to change how.

Low temperature makes the model predictable, high temperature makes it creative and riskier. Top-p keeps only the likeliest tokens whose chances add up to p.

Step 13Answer your prompt

Repeat until done

The chosen token is added to the input, and the whole model runs again to pick the next one. Token by token the answer appears, until the model outputs a special end token.

That's why answers stream in word by word. A memory called the KV cache saves earlier work, so each new token is quick.

The whole journey

Collect and clean the text. Train a model to predict the next token, then teach it to be a helpful assistant.

For every prompt: turn words into numbers, send them through the layers, and pick one token at a time.
